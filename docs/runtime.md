# Runtime and boot

Code: `src/main.ts`, `src/app/`, `src/core/`.

- **Entry points.** `main.ts` gates the platform and imports `app/boot.ts` dynamically. The whole startup is one promise chain with a terminal catch through `fatal.report`. `Game` (`app/game.ts`) only owns the camera, player, pipeline and frame loop. World assembly lives in `app/world.ts`, and each system lives in its own module.
- **Boot stages:**
  - renderer;
  - atmosphere (atmosphere LUTs and the star glare table in one worker, noise volumes in another);
  - environment (async: the star illuminant measurement, then the environment capture);
  - compile;
  - warmup;
  - first frame;
  - running.
  Each stage is logged with its duration.
- **The city** (`src/city/`): built inside the environment stage (geometry on the main thread, ~1–2 s); its interior environment capture needs the frame, so it follows the open-sky capture. `Game` runs `city.update(dt, feet)` every frame (the gate's airlock; the player's walls follow the leaves) and sets the glare's star visibility at the eye.
- **The field** (`src/field/`, `docs/field.md`): built in the environment stage after the city: geometry (~1 s), its walls, the star-shade (raster and a compute pass) and its reflection probes (one capture per zone after the interior capture, sharing its sky material). `World.update` poses the field's movers, steps its relief vents and advances the probes' round-robin refresh.
- **The house and the estate** (`docs/house.md`, `docs/estate.md`): the house's exterior loads from R2 in parallel with the atmosphere stage; the house and the estate (~4 s) are built in the environment stage after the field, their walk meshes join the city's, their occluders join the star-shade, and the estate's probe is captured after the field's. The floors stream in once the game runs. Each frame `Game` updates the doors (E), decides whether the eye is indoors, culls the outdoors to the windows and pauses outdoor probe refreshes while it is.
- **The Stand and its terminal** (`docs/terminal.md`): built in the environment stage after the house and the estate (~0.1 s); its walk mesh joins the city's, its walls join the car's, its occluders the star-shade, and its floor the shade's floor map; its probe is captured after the estate's. Each frame `Game` hands E to the terminal first (`terminal/use.ts`), then the car, then a door, and updates the OS and its screen (`Stand.update`). The narration loads after boot.
- **The vehicles** (`docs/vehicles.md`): built in the environment stage after the house and the estate (~0.5 s), lit once the field's probes exist. Each frame `Game` hands the frame to `app/driving.ts` (walking, getting in, driving, getting out), then `World.vehicles.update` flies the craft (the East Lock follows it in `city.update`) and sets their shade on the pad.
- **The crew** (`docs/crew.md`): built in the environment stage after the occluder raster (~1.6 s): its routes plan on the field's occupancy (shared with the walls), its fliers on the raster's height map; its fixtures then join the raster and the walls. Its look takes the field's and the estate's probes. Each frame `Game` updates it after the vehicles (`crew/frame.ts`: the walker or, driving, the car; when the craft next comes in; the eye), and its shade multiplies into the platform's and the estate's.
- **Audio** (`docs/audio.md`): the audio bank is synthesised in a worker alongside the atmosphere stage (~2 s); `GameAudio` is built in the compile stage from `app/audioWorld.ts` (what is underfoot, the house, the trees). Its context stays suspended until the pointer-lock click; pause suspends it and fatal errors close it.
- **The intro** (`docs/intro-film.md`): its nine lines load with the atmosphere stage (`intro/audio/lines.ts`); `Intro` (its ship, its view of the sphere from outside) is built after the world, its sound once `GameAudio` exists. The first Enter starts it; `Game.introFrame` runs it (the world advances only on the band), and the game spawns on its last frame. Enter skips.
- **Pause** freezes the frame loop (no world time, no renders) until the pointer is locked again (`docs/ui.md`). `Game` updates it each frame after the camera's matrix.
- **Ordering constraints:**
  - Noise volumes must be set before any star shader is built.
  - The space backdrop is baked (and the frame angle set) before the sky is built.
  - The illuminant must be measured before the environment capture.
  - White-balance gains are set when the pipeline is created.
- **Hidden-tab frames.** `nextFrame` falls back to a 100 ms timeout, because requestAnimationFrame stalls in hidden panes during boot.
- **GPU errors.** Device-loss and error callbacks are installed before `renderer.init()`, chained to Three's defaults. Any GPU error during boot aborts startup. Fatal runtime errors stop the loop and show the entry screen's error panel.
- **Viewport.** DPR policy and resize coalescing are in `core/viewport.ts`, following the project rules.
- **Default spawn** is the `gate` viewpoint (design §7.4). `?gate=closed|outer|inner` holds the West Gate's airlock in a state for review; `?lock=closed|outer|inner|open` poses the East Lock; `?vent` lifts the Cold Hold's largest relief stack. Field review viewpoints: `reveal`, `porch`, `resonator`, `arrival`, `drive` (city_plan §8) and `gyre`, `hold`, `stills`, `converter`, `reckoner`, `loom`, `mast`, `halo`, `lock`. Gate review viewpoints: `portal`, `chamber`, `court`; Dome: `drum`, `inside`, `ringwalk`; vehicles: `car`, `pad`; the Stand: `stand`, `standrim`, `terminal`; house: `front`, `parlour`, `hall`, `library`, `dining`, `landing`, `study`, `turret`; estate: `gateway`, `balustrade`, `forecourt`, `approach`, `pond`, `kitchen`, `carriage`, `back`. `tools/render.mjs --one-boot` reuses one boot for several spots (spots may also be `x,z,yawDeg,pitchDeg`), `--verbose` prints the page's log.
- **Debug URL parameters** (`debug/params.ts`): `spot`, `view` (incl. `glare`), `sky` (incl. `space`), `tm`, `ev`, `time`, `fov`, `look`, `ao`, `probe`, `profile`, `autostart`, `wobble`, `steady`, `manual`, `craft=<leg>[:s]` (run the craft's visit forward; with `time` it holds still), `drive` (start in the car), `terminal=<keys>` (start at the terminal's screen, pressing the keys after its boot), `tat=<s>` (hold the recording playing at s seconds in), `crew=<type>[:i[:s]]` (run the crew s seconds on and face robot i; with `time` the crew holds still), `intro=<s>[:hold]` (play the intro from s seconds in at once; `:hold` holds it there), `introView=<lat,lon,dist>` (frame the intro's first scene from elsewhere), `introReview` (the intro alone, looping, with hold, scrub and scene keys: `docs/intro-film.md`). `tools/probe-hdr.mjs` reads back scene radiance at screen points for calibration.
  - **Local hosts only** (`isDebugHost`: localhost, loopback, `*.localhost`). On a deployed host every parameter is ignored and `window.__dyson` is not installed, so the review tools only work against a local server.
  - The sky debug mode is set after the environment capture, so debug views keep the normal lighting.
- **No dev server.** `render.mjs` and `fps.mjs` take `--dist <dir>` to load a `vite build` with nothing listening on a port: the browser's requests to http://localhost are answered from the build by request interception (`tools/serveDist.mjs`).
- **Headless renders.** `node tools/render.mjs [--query ...] spot…` renders fixed viewpoints (`debug/viewpoints.ts`). It prints exposure, the star illuminant and GPU time (with `profile`). The first render of a batch runs slow (GPU warm-up); profile with a warm-up spot first.
- **Motion clips.** `node tools/clip.mjs --seconds 20 --query "fov=24&look=20,47" spot` boots once with `?manual`, steps real game time (four TAA sub-frames per captured frame) and encodes an MP4 with ffmpeg (`--speed` scales game time, `--start` jumps the clock).
- **Cinematic clips.** `node tools/cinematic/cinematic.mjs` renders the shot list offline at 3443×2160, 30 fps, from a separate capture build with the LOD and drawing-buffer cap lifted (`docs/cinematic.md`).
- **Frame rate.** `node tools/fps.mjs spot…` counts uncapped frames of the real loop (vsync off, frozen star time). The `?profile` timestamp sum overstates pipelines of many small passes on tile-based GPUs, and costs depend on what is on screen (an erupting prominence, say): compare at a fixed `time`.
- **UI captures.** `node tools/ui-shots.mjs --dist <dir>` captures loading, ready and paused, and checks that pause holds the loop.
- **Terminal screen stills.** `node tools/terminal-screens/shots.mjs [keys[@s]…]` draws the West Gate terminal's screen alone (its OS and drawing code in Chrome, no world) after pressing keys, a recording held s seconds in, to `tools/out/terminal/`; with no spec, every page and the briefings' beats, in seconds (`docs/terminal.md`).
- **Audio preview.** `node tools/audio-preview.mjs [scenario…]` renders scripted walks through the airlock (or `direction`) offline in headless Chrome to `tools/out/audio/*.wav`, with event logs and levels.
- **Intro review.** `node tools/intro-shots.mjs --dist <dir> t…` (film stills), `node tools/ship-shots/shots.mjs [--real] spec…` (the ship alone, in seconds), `node tools/intro-audio.mjs` (the soundtrack), `node tools/run-ts.mjs <file.ts>` (runs a TypeScript script, for measurements).
- **Flicker meter.** `node tools/flicker.mjs --wobble 3 --steady spot…` boots with `?manual`, steps frames by hand while the camera sweeps its yaw (37-frame period, ±3° ≈ 10 px/frame at the turn), and captures the frame at the same phase of every sweep. The view is identical, so per-pixel differences are temporal instability. It prints mean and 99.9th-percentile σ and writes a heat map.
  - With `--steady` the camera speed at each capture is identical too, and only the TAA jitter phase differs: this isolates aliasing and crawl.
  - Without it, TAA's normal softening under motion is mixed in, and high-contrast detail inflates the numbers.
  - A WebGPU canvas is only readable in the task that rendered it, so the capture happens right after `renderFrame`.
